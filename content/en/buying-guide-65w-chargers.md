## How to Choose a 65W Wall Charger

65W chargers are the sweet spot for laptops, tablets and fast-charging phones. When stocking them for your market, work through these points:

- **Plug type per market** — US (Type A), EU (Type C), UK (Type G), AU (Type I) and IN (Type D/M) versions are all available. Order the plug variant for your destination market — never mix them in one shipment.
- **Fast-charging protocols** — USB-C Power Delivery (PD 3.0) is the standard for laptops and modern phones; PPS support adds optimized charging for Samsung and other PPS devices; QC 3.0 covers older Android phones.
- **Port configuration** — single USB-C keeps cost and size down; dual-port (USB-C + USB-A) versions serve more devices and sell better in retail.
- **Output power** — 65W charges a 13–14" laptop, tablet and phone; check the per-port output table if you choose multi-port models so each device gets enough power.
- **Safety and build** — look for over-current, over-voltage, over-temperature and short-circuit protection, plus flame-retardant shells. These reduce warranty claims and safety complaints.
- **Certifications for your market** — CE (EU), FCC (US), PSE (Japan), KC (South Korea), UL, SAA (Australia) and BIS (India) are required or expected in different regions. Confirm which certificates your market needs before ordering.
- **OEM/ODM options** — logo printing, custom colors, retail packaging, cable length and plug pins can all be customized by YiwuYimei.

## Key Specifications Explained

- **USB-C Power Delivery (PD)** — a fast-charging standard that negotiates voltage and current with the device; 65W PD charges a typical 13–14" laptop at full speed and fast-charges phones.
- **PPS (Programmable Power Supply)** — an extension of PD 3.0 that adjusts output in small steps; needed for the fastest charging on Samsung Galaxy and other PPS-enabled devices.
- **GaN vs standard silicon** — GaN (gallium nitride) chargers deliver the same power in a smaller, lighter body with less heat; standard silicon is cheaper and perfectly adequate for most programs.
- **Wattage math** — a 65W charger can run a 45W laptop and still have headroom; it also fast-charges phones at their negotiated rate (18–45W typical) without overloading.
- **Plug standards** — each market has its own plug geometry; the internal electronics are identical, so only the plug variant changes per destination.
