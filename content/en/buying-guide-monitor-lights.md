## How to Choose a Monitor Light Bar

Monitor light bars are an office-and-gamer category: they sell year-round and win on ergonomics claims. Cover these points before ordering:

- **Mounting and fit** — screen-clips must fit monitor thickness 1–6cm typically; curved-monitor compatibility is a question retail buyers increasingly ask.
- **Color temperature range** — 2900–6000K tunable (warm to cool white) is the standard; fixed-temperature models cost less but sell worse in office channels.
- **CRI (Color Rendering Index)** — CRI >80 is the baseline for eye-comfort claims; CRI >90 supports premium positioning.
- **Dimming and control** — touch dimming is standard across our range; stepless brightness with memory function differentiates premium SKUs.
- **Power** — DC 5V USB-A or USB-C is universal; no mains wiring means simpler certifications and lower shipping cost.
- **Light distribution** — asymmetric front projection lights the desk, not the screen — verify the cut-off angle to avoid glare claims being disproved in reviews.

## Key Specifications Explained

- **Illuminance** — 300–800 lux at desk level is the ergonomic target; spec sheets should state lux at a given distance.
- **Power draw** — 2.5–5W typical; USB-powered models never exceed 5W so any USB port works.
- **Material** — ABS body with aluminum-alloy accents on premium models; silicone-padded clips protect the monitor bezel.
- **Size classes** — 40–45cm bars suit 27"+ monitors; compact 30cm versions fit home-office setups.
- **Retail packaging** — color boxes with window displays well; include USB-C cable in-box for the premium tier.

## Positioning Tips for Sellers

- **Office/professional channel** — lead with eye-comfort, CRI and flicker-free claims; certifications matter (check local market requirements).
- **Gamer channel** — lead with RGB ambient options (available on selected models) and screen-glare elimination.
- **Bundle potential** — monitor bars pair naturally with monitor arms and desk accessories; mixed-carton programs start at 200 pcs.
