## How a Portable Fan Is Built - and Where Cheap Ones Fail

Portable fans look like a commodity until the first summer return wave. YiwuYimei builds handheld, desk, neck-worn and stroller fans, and the difference between a fan that survives the season and one that doesn't comes down to three things most catalog photos cannot show:

**Housing and blades - injection molding in-house.** Our own injection shop molds the housings and blade assemblies, so blade balance (the main source of rattle and noise complaints) is controlled at the mold level, not patched at final assembly. Custom colors and shapes are a mold change, not a compromise.

**Motors and batteries - where failures actually happen.** Fan returns are overwhelmingly motor whine, battery swelling and switch failures - all component-level decisions made long before assembly. We spec brushless or quality brushed motors by price tier, and battery packs are matched to the load with protection circuitry; unbranded cells with no protection board are the classic corner cut you should always ask about.

**Battery safety and compliance.** Powered fans ship with lithium batteries, which means transport rules and product safety rules both apply. We arrange UN38.3 test summaries for air freight, plus CE, FCC, UKCA, PSE (Japan) and KC (Korea) documentation by market - ask at quoting stage so paperwork lands with the goods.
